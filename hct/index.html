Compressibility, Turbulence and High Speed Flow↗

Thomas B. Gatski · Jean-Paul Bonnet

The backbone of this reading path: governing equations, density-weighted variables, and prediction strategies.

Scope & reading cues

Read Chapters 2 and 3 for the equations, then Chapter 5 for DNS, LES, and RANS. Derive the terms before comparing closure models. The second edition was released in 2013; the shop also lists a later product date.

Large Eddy Simulation for Compressible Flows↗

Eric Garnier · Nikolaus Adams · Pierre Sagaut

Connect the filtered equations to subgrid modeling, numerical discretization, and practical compressible LES.

Scope & reading cues

Begin with the LES governing equations, then follow the relationship between the subgrid model and numerical discretization. Keep explicit filtering, implicit filtering, and numerical dissipation distinct.

Sanjiva K. Lele

A foundational review for organizing the physical effects that sit under the word compressibility.

Scope & reading cues

Read as a historical map, then pair it with current DNS and scaling papers. Mean-property variation, pressure-driven dilatation, and shock interactions are different questions.

Scaling · foundation2016

Mean velocity scaling for compressible wall turbulence with heat transfer↗

Andrew Trettel · Johan Larsson

A reference point for following later work on velocity transformations with variable density and viscosity.

Scope & reading cues

A foundational comparison, not a claim about the latest state of the art. Record the thermal condition and geometry before comparing transformed profiles; a mean-profile collapse does not establish agreement in every statistic.

Reproduce a profileData

Compressible boundary-layer DNS profiles↗

Johan Larsson · Computational Turbulence Laboratory

A directly usable starting point: averaged profiles and input files for eight compressible boundary-layer cases.

Scope & reading cues

The source covers Mach 2–10 and cold, adiabatic, and heated walls with a calorically perfect gas. These are averaged profiles, not full instantaneous fields. Cite Kumar and Larsson (PRF 2025) as requested by the data page; check its README and normalization.

12
Validate wall statistics2018

NASA supersonic & hypersonic flat-plate DNS↗

Chao Zhang · Lian Duan · Meelan M. Choudhari

Download high-speed flat-plate statistics and turbulent kinetic energy budgets from the relocated Turbulence Modeling Resource.

Scope & reading cues

The source spans nominal freestream Mach 2.5–14 and wall-to-recovery temperature ratios 0.18–1.0, with no high-enthalpy effects included. These are statistics and budget files, not raw three-dimensional snapshots. Each file begins with detailed README information. Companion paper: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7802740/
